### Document Purpose

This note explains **how capital payback (استرداد رأس المال) is assessed** for the SAR 100 million entry: what payback means here, how it is measured on a cumulative-distribution basis, how it reads across scenarios, and how it relates to operating performance and revenue validation. It is a **methodology note**, not a payback promise. **All figures are illustrative and model-derived; there is no guaranteed return and no fixed payback date.**

---

### Executive Summary

Payback is measured as the point at which **cumulative Investor Distributions (توزيعات الشريك الاستثماري) equal the SAR 100 million Entry Capital (رأس مال الدخول)**. It is a distribution-based measure, distinct from the model's **operating break-even (Year 1 base)**, which marks when channel net cash covers OPEX and depreciation — a different question. On a preliminary base-case reading, a payback path can be **studied around Year 8**, conditional on channels converting to actual contracts and on an adopted distribution policy. Conservative pushes this later; Expansion brings it earlier.

### Definition of Payback — تعريف الاسترداد

**Payback** is the horizon over which the Strategic Partner recovers the SAR 100m entry through cumulative distributions. It is **not** an accounting break-even, **not** an NPV, and **not** a guaranteed date. It answers one question: *by when do cumulative distributions to the Partner equal the capital deployed?*

### Payback Calculation Logic — منطق حساب الاسترداد

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| Baseline | SAR 100 million Entry Capital. |
| Inflow | Investor Distributions (30% of distributable window cash), per Document 04. |
| Method | **Cumulative-distribution method** — sum distributions year by year until they reach the baseline. |
| Payback point | First year in which cumulative distributions ≥ SAR 100 million. |
| Distinction | Separate from **operating break-even (Year 1 base)**. |

```
Cumulative distributions (Σ yearly 30% share)  ≥  SAR 100m  →  Payback reached
```

### Payback Drivers — محركات الاسترداد

| Driver | Effect on payback |
|---|---|
| Channel conversion speed (revenue validation) | Faster conversion → earlier payback. |
| Distribution policy | Reserve-first and cadence shift timing. |
| Operating performance | Higher margins → faster cumulative distributions. |
| Reserve requirements | Larger reserves delay distributable cash. |
| Scenario levers | Discount/revenue/OPEX factors move the whole path. |

### Relationship Between Payback and Operating Performance

Payback is downstream of operating performance: distributions can only accumulate once the window generates operating cash and funds reserves. Strong operating performance (margins holding near the ~46% blended level, channels activating on schedule) accelerates cumulative distributions; weak performance delays them.

### Relationship Between Payback and Revenue Validation

Payback is only as reliable as the revenue behind it. Because channels are **verifiable, not contracted**, the payback reading is explicitly conditional on channels converting to **actual contracts and execution**. Until revenue is validated, the payback path is a modeled possibility, not a schedule.
